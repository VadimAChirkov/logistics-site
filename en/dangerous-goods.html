Carriage of dangerous goods

Dangerous goods are carried by all modes of transport under special rules. Below is general information (classification, admission to carriage, obligations of the parties) and materials for each mode of transport.

What counts as dangerous goods

Dangerous goods are substances, materials, articles and production waste which, owing to their inherent properties and characteristics, may during carriage endanger human life and health, harm the environment, or cause damage to or destruction of material assets and vehicles.

Classification of dangerous goods

Dangerous goods are divided into 9 classes according to their physical and chemical properties and the nature of the hazard:

Class 1Explosive substances and articlesindustrial explosives, pyrotechnics, ammunition
Class 2Gases (compressed, liquefied, dissolved)propane, oxygen, chlorine, ammonia
Class 3Flammable liquidspetrol, solvents, alcohols
Class 4.1–4.3Flammable solids; substances liable to spontaneous combustion; substances which, in contact with water, emit flammable gasessulphur, coal, calcium carbide

Admission to carriage

  • Only dangerous goods listed in the relevant list (the Dangerous Goods List, the ADR/IMDG list, etc.) and assigned a UN number may be accepted for carriage.
  • Each dangerous good must have a proper shipping name and UN number stated in the transport documents.
  • Carriage is permitted only in sound packaging appropriate to the hazard class, with the required marking and hazard labels.
  • Packing different dangerous goods together in one package is permitted only if they are compatible.

Obligations of the consignor

  • Correctly classify the goods (determine the class, UN number, proper shipping name).
  • Provide proper packaging, packing, marking and hazard labels.
  • Complete the transport documents and attach the necessary accompanying documents (emergency instructions, dangerous goods declaration, etc.).
  • Confirm that the goods are ready for carriage and comply with the rules.

Obligations of the carrier

  • Accept the goods for carriage only if they comply with the rules.
  • Ensure carriage conditions that exclude danger (securing, compatibility, escort).
  • Comply with the requirements for the vehicle and for operations involving dangerous goods.

MSDS — Material Safety Data Sheet

The MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet), in Russia — the Safety Data Sheet for chemical products, is the principal document containing full information on the properties and hazards of a chemical substance (product) and the measures for its safe handling.

Purpose

The safety data sheet ensures safe handling of chemical products at every stage of the life cycle: production, storage, transport, use and disposal. It is needed for correct classification of dangerous goods, selection of packaging and marking, staff training and emergency response.

Content (16 sections under GHS)

Under the international standard (GHS; in Russia — GOST 30333) the safety data sheet consists of 16 sections:

  1. Identification of the substance/mixture and of the supplier — name, use, supplier contacts.
  2. Hazards identification — hazard classification, signal word, pictograms, H-statements.
  3. Composition / information on ingredients — chemical composition, CAS numbers, concentrations.
  4. First-aid measures — actions in case of inhalation, skin/eye contact, ingestion.
  5. Fire-fighting measures — suitable and unsuitable extinguishing media.
  6. Accidental release measures — containment, clean-up, personnel protection.
  7. Handling and storage — safe working and storage conditions.
  8. Exposure controls / personal protection — exposure limits, personal protective equipment.
  9. Physical and chemical properties — state, temperatures, solubility, etc.
  10. Stability and reactivity — conditions to avoid.
  11. Toxicological information — routes of exposure, symptoms, acute/chronic toxicity.
  12. Ecological information — environmental impact.
  13. Disposal considerations — methods of neutralising and disposing of waste.
  14. Transport information — UN number, class, packing group, transport requirements.
  15. Regulatory information — references to applicable regulations and standards.
  16. Other information — date of issue/revision, changes.

How to use it

The safety data sheet is read "from identification to action": first determine the hazard (sections 1–3), then protection and response (4–8), then properties and effects (9–12), and finally transport and regulatory aspects (13–16). For carriage, the key sections are 2 (classification), 9 (properties) and 14 (transport information): it is on their basis that the consignor classifies the goods, selects packaging and completes the transport documents.

Where it is used

  • Classification of dangerous goods — assignment to a class and UN number.
  • Arranging carriage — MSDS data is transferred into the transport documents and the dangerous goods declaration.
  • Selection of packaging, marking and hazard labels.
  • Staff training and emergency preparedness.
  • Customs clearance and compliance with technical regulations (TR CU 019/2011, REACH, etc.).
Practical tip: when shipping dangerous goods, the key is accurate classification (class + UN number). Packaging, marking, compatibility and carriage conditions — and ultimately safety and the absence of claims — all depend on it. A classification error is the most common and most costly mistake when handling dangerous goods.
