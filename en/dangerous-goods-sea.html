Sea carriage of dangerous goods

Sea carriage of dangerous goods is governed by the International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code (IMDG Code), adopted within the framework of the SOLAS Convention, as well as Chapter VII of the SOLAS-74 Convention and MARPOL (as regards marine pollution).

The IMDG Code

The IMDG Code is a set of rules for the safe sea carriage of dangerous goods in packaged form. It is mandatory for SOLAS contracting states and applied by carriers worldwide. It defines:

  • the classification of dangerous goods (9 classes, harmonised with the UN Model Regulations);
  • requirements for packing and packaging (packing groups I, II, III by degree of danger);
  • marking and hazard labels;
  • stowage and segregation rules (separation of incompatible goods on board);
  • documentation (dangerous goods declaration, transport document).

Packing groups

By degree of danger, goods are divided into three packing groups:

GroupDegree of danger
Ihigh danger (X)
IImedium danger (Y)
IIIlow danger (Z)

Dangerous goods declaration

The consignor must provide the carrier with a dangerous goods declaration (DGD), which contains:

  • the proper shipping name of the goods and the UN number;
  • the class (division) of danger;
  • the packing group;
  • the quantity and type of packaging;
  • a confirmation that the goods are properly classified, packed, marked and fit for carriage.

Container and marking

A freight container with dangerous goods must bear hazard labels, the UN number, and, for bulk goods, an orange plate. For containers in sea traffic, the VGM rules (verified gross mass) and sealing also apply.

Practical tip: for a sea shipment of dangerous goods, agree in advance with the line on the possibility of carriage (not all lines accept all classes) and prepare the DGD before the container is placed. An error in the DGD is a common cause of refusal to load and container downtime.
