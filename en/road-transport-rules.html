Rules for the Carriage of Goods by Road (Government Resolution No. 2200)

The Rules for the Carriage of Goods by Road are approved by Government Resolution No. 2200 of 21 December 2020 (in force since 1 January 2021). This is the principal subordinate act detailing the procedure for domestic carriage of goods by road in Russia: from the conclusion of the contract of carriage to the delivery of the goods and the filing of claims. The Rules are issued in pursuance of the Road Transport Charter (Federal Law No. 259-FZ of 08.11.2007).

What the Rules govern

Resolution No. 2200 establishes the procedure for organising the carriage of goods by road, including:

  • conclusion of a contract of carriage of goods and a charter contract (freight contract);
  • the procedure for providing vehicles and containers;
  • tendering and acceptance of goods, loading and unloading;
  • determination of the weight of the goods and sealing;
  • delivery times and delivery of the goods;
  • specifics of carrying particular types of goods;
  • the procedure for drawing up reports and filing claims.

The consignment note (TN)

Carriage of goods under a contract of carriage is formalised by a consignment note (TN). Its form is approved by the Rules (Annex No. 4). Key rules:

  • The TN is drawn up by the consignor (unless the contract of carriage provides otherwise) for each carriage.
  • The note is made out in three copies: for the consignor, the consignee and the carrier.
  • The TN is signed by the consignor and the carrier; the signatures record the taking over of the goods for carriage.
  • It contains particulars of the consignor, consignee and carrier, the goods (description, number of packages, weight, declared value), the route, times and conditions of carriage.
Link with the ETrN: from 01.09.2026 the consignment note is generated as an electronic consignment note (ETrN). A paper TN is permitted only in the cases established by law. For details, see the road transport documents section.

Acceptance, loading and unloading

  • Loading of the goods onto the vehicle is, as a general rule, performed by the consignor, and unloading by the consignee, unless the contract provides otherwise.
  • The carrier must provide a sound vehicle suitable for carrying the relevant goods within the agreed time.
  • The weight of the goods is determined by the consignor (by weighing or calculation); the parties record it in the note.
  • Goods requiring containers/packing are tendered in sound containers ensuring their preservation during carriage.
  • After loading, the vehicle may be sealed — the seals are recorded in the note.

Delivery times

The time for delivery of the goods is determined by the contract of carriage. Where no time is fixed by the contract, the goods must be delivered within a reasonable time. For breach of the delivery time the carrier bears liability (a penalty for delay under the Road Transport Charter).

Delivery of the goods

The goods are delivered to the consignee named in the note at the destination. On delivery, the weight, number of packages and condition of the goods are checked. Where loss, shortage or damage to the goods, or a breach of the seals, is found on delivery, a report is drawn up — this is the basis for a subsequent claim and action.

Reports: the procedure for drawing them up

Reports are drawn up in circumstances that may give rise to the liability of the parties, in particular:

  • loss, shortage, or damage to (spoilage of) the goods;
  • failure to provide a vehicle or container;
  • delay in delivery of the goods;
  • delay in loading/unloading (excessive demurrage).

A report is drawn up in the presence of the parties' representatives and signed by them; where a party evades signing, a note to that effect is made. The report is attached to the claim.

Claims and actions

As a rule, a claim is presented to the carrier before an action is brought. The procedure and time limits are established by the Road Transport Charter (259-FZ):

  • a claim against the carrier is presented within the established time limits (for example, for loss of goods — after the delivery time);
  • the carrier must consider the claim and reply within 30 days;
  • the limitation period for claims arising from the carriage of goods by road is one year.

Liability of the parties

The liability of the carrier, consignor and consignee is established by the Road Transport Charter (259-FZ). The main provisions:

  • For loss/shortage of the goods, the carrier compensates the value of the lost (missing) goods.
  • For damage to the goods — the amount by which their value has declined.
  • For delay in delivery — a penalty, as a rule, as a percentage of the carriage charge for each day of delay, but not exceeding the amount of the carriage charge.
  • The consignor/consignee are liable for vehicle demurrage and other breaches provided for by the contract and the law.
Practical significance: Resolution No. 2200 applies to domestic carriage by road within Russia. For international carriage, the CMR Convention applies (plus a TIR Carnet for customs transit).
