Exporting concentrates containing precious metals

Precious-metal concentrate is a good restricted for export from the EAEU. Its export requires a one-off licence from the Ministry of Industry and Trade, preceded by approval from the Ministry of Finance. This fundamentally sets it apart from, for example, copper concentrate, where no licence is needed.

Three layers of regulation

  1. Non-tariff — export licensing (EEC Board Decision No. 30 of 21.04.2015, section 2.10).
  2. Criminal law — since 10.08.2026 concentrate (code 2616) is on the list of strategically important goods (RF Government Resolution No. 923); smuggling = Art. 226.1 of the Criminal Code.
  3. Sanctions — the EU/US/UK/Japan/Switzerland/Canada have banned imports of Russian gold; shipments are rerouted to Asia.

1. Regulation: who controls what

The base law is Federal Law No. 41-FZ of 26.03.1998 "On Precious Metals and Precious Stones".

2. Licensing: the EAEU permit procedure

Basis — EEC Board Decision No. 30 of 21.04.2015 "On non-tariff regulation", section 2.10 (tables 1 and 2).

The licence type is one-off (tied to a specific contract and volume, valid up to 1 year). The general licence applies only to refined bullion.

Current approval procedure — RF Ministry of Finance Order No. 10n of 16.02.2026:

  • effective from 01.09.2026 to 01.09.2032;
  • replaced the former Order No. 50n of 29.03.2019 (no longer in force).

Steps for the exporter:

  1. Assay report on precious-metal content — an accredited laboratory; sampling by the territorial body of Rosstandart.
  2. Foreign trade contract — indicating mass, Au content and associated precious metals, price. The contract is required even for export for laboratory testing.
  3. Ministry of Finance conclusion on the impossibility/inexpediency of industrial extraction + approval of the licence application.
  4. One-off licence from the Ministry of Industry and Trade.
  5. Customs clearance at a specialized post of the Central Excise Customs.

Licence fee — 7,500 rubles; review period — 15 working days; filing — electronically via the "non-tariff" system (non-tariff.gov.ru) with a digital signature.

3. Concentrate pricing (general scheme)

The concentrate is sold not by gross weight but by metal content. The base formula:

Cost = (Au in concentrate, g/t × mass × recovery factor × Au price)
       − treatment charge (TC)
       − refining charge (RC)
       − penalties for harmful impurities (As, Hg, Sb, etc.)
  • Content × mass — how much metal is physically in the lot (e.g. 30 g/t × 1000 t = 30 kg Au).
  • Recovery factor (payability) — usually 90–95% of the calculated content is paid.
  • TC (treatment charge) — the processor's fee for processing the concentrate.
  • RC (refining charge) — the fee for refining.
  • Penalties — deductions for harmful impurities (arsenic As, mercury Hg, antimony Sb).

The transaction price is the result of negotiation over the formula. The economics depend directly on concentrate quality and TC/RC rates, which the market revises depending on the scarcity of processing capacity.

4. Currency control: repatriation and contract accounting

It is important to separate three things that are often confused:

  1. Contract registration with the bank — in force. The contract is registered and supporting documents are submitted.
  2. Mandatory sale of FX proceeds — suspended. Presidential Decree No. 529 of 28.06.2022 suspended parts 1 and 2 of Art. 19 of Federal Law No. 173-FZ; ratios have been zeroed since 22.08.2025.
  3. Repatriation — for a number of goods partially suspended/reduced (since 22.08.2025 the rate has been reduced to 0%).
Practical outcome as of November 2026: there is no hard requirement to sell or repatriate proceeds, but contract registration, deadlines and supporting documents remain. Fines under Art. 15.25 of the Administrative Code apply in the non-revoked part.

Classification rule: ore/concentrate with gold (+ associated precious metals) → 2616 90 000 0; silver only → 2616 10 000 0. Typical content of a gold-bearing flotation concentrate is Au ~15–65 g/t.

  • Export duty — 0% (RF Government Resolution No. 754 of 30.08.2013).
  • Declaration fee — RF Government Resolution No. 863 of 28.12.2004.
  • Clearance — a specialized post of the Central Excise Customs.

When declaring, the licence details (section 2.10) are stated in box 44 of the declaration.

6. VAT: 0% on export

The 0% rate applies under subpara. 1 para. 1 Art. 164 of the RF Tax Code — sale of goods exported under the customs procedure of export. The exporter gains the right to deduct input VAT but must document the 0% rate:

  • foreign trade contract;
  • customs declaration with release/export marks;
  • transport and shipping documents;
  • bank documents (receipt of proceeds).

The confirmation period and the list of documents are per Art. 165 of the RF Tax Code.

7. Risks and common mistakes

The main legal risk is smuggling of strategically important goods:

  • RF Government Resolution No. 923 of 13.09.2012 (as amended 10.08.2026) — list of strategically important goods for Art. 226.1 of the Criminal Code.
  • Item 14(1): "Precious-metal ores and concentrates" — code 2616.
  • Illegal movement across the EAEU border = Art. 226.1 of the Criminal Code, up to 7 years of imprisonment. Large size — 1 million rubles.
