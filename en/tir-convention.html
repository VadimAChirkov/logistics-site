TIR Convention (1975)

The TIR Convention (from French Transports Internationaux Routiers) is the Customs Convention on the International Transport of Goods under Cover of TIR Carnets, concluded in Geneva on 14 November 1975 (entered into force on 20 March 1978). Russia is a party to the Convention; the national guaranteeing association is ASMAP (the Association of International Road Carriers).

How it works

The TIR Convention allows goods to be carried across several borders without intermediate customs inspection: the goods move in a sealed approved vehicle from the customs office of departure to the customs office of destination. Duties and charges are neither paid nor deposited en route — instead an international guarantee system operates.

  • The goods are carried under customs seals and marks and, as a rule, are not inspected at intermediate customs offices.
  • Import/export duties and charges are not paid or deposited at customs offices en route.
  • It is used for transit carriage (goods move through the territory of states without being released into free circulation).

The TIR Carnet

The TIR Carnet is the customs transit document under which goods are carried under the TIR procedure. It is issued for a specific carriage (one vehicle/combination, one route) and consists of a cover, a set of detached vouchers and counterfoils. The carnet is issued to the carrier by the guaranteeing association (in Russia, ASMAP) after the carrier has been approved for the TIR system.

Distribution of sheets under paper processing

A standard TIR Carnet consists of a cover, sets of sheets (a detached voucher + a counterfoil) and an accident report. Each "voucher + counterfoil" pair has its own colour and purpose. As the carnet passes through customs offices, the sheets are distributed as follows:

SheetColourWhere it goes
Coveryellowstays in the carnet; contains the holder's data, validity and the guaranteeing association
Voucher No. 1 (goods)whiteremains at the customs office of departure (customs record of the goods)
Voucher No. 1 (guarantee)greenremains at the customs office of departure and is sent to the guaranteeing association (starts the guarantee chain)
Counterfoil No. 1—stays in the carnet
Voucher No. 2 (goods)whiteremains at the customs office of destination
Voucher No. 2 (guarantee)greenremains at the customs office of destination and is sent to the guaranteeing association (closes the guarantee chain)
Counterfoil No. 2—stays in the carnet
Additional sheets (where needed)yellowused by intermediate customs offices in the event of transloading/transfer
Accident report—completed in the event of a road accident or irregularity
The logic of distribution: the white vouchers are for the customs record (they remain at the customs offices of departure and destination); the green vouchers are for the guarantee chain (the customs office sends them to the guaranteeing association, which uses them to verify that the TIR operation has been properly terminated and that the guarantee may be discharged). The counterfoils stay in the carnet and, after the operation, are returned to the holder — proof that the TIR carriage has been completed.

Guarantee chain and the guarantee limit

Each TIR Carnet is covered by a guarantee of the national guaranteeing association (in Russia, ASMAP), acting in conjunction with the international union IRU and insurance companies. Where obligations to pay duties/charges arise during a TIR carriage and the carrier does not settle them, the guaranteeing association is answerable to the customs authorities.

The international TIR guarantee limit is up to €100,000 per carnet (raised from the former €60,000). This amount is the ceiling of the guarantee chain's liability for a single TIR operation; the actual guarantee for a given carriage depends on the goods, the route and the amount of security.

Approval of the carrier and the vehicle

  • Carrier approval for the TIR system: the carrier must meet the Convention's requirements (reliability, no serious customs offences) and obtain a certificate of approval through the national guaranteeing association (ASMAP).
  • Vehicle approval: the load compartment (container) must be constructed so that goods cannot be removed from or introduced into it without visible breaking of the seals. An approved vehicle receives a certificate of approval of a road vehicle for the carriage of goods under customs seals.

eTIR — the electronic TIR Carnet

eTIR is the digital version of the TIR Carnet, in which customs offices and guaranteeing associations exchange carriage data electronically (instead of paper vouchers). Its legal basis is Annex 11 to the TIR Convention (in force since 2021).

As of 2026, eTIR is at the stage of pilot application and gradual expansion rather than full replacement of paper carnets: countries (including the members of the Organization of Turkic States) are extending the use of eTIR on individual routes, and pilot runs with the electronic e-CMR consignment note into Russia are planned for late 2026. The paper TIR Carnet remains the primary document on most routes.

Russia's participation

The Russian Federation is a party to the TIR Convention. The national guaranteeing association is ASMAP, which issues TIR Carnets, approves carriers for the system and liaises with the customs authorities (the Federal Customs Service of Russia) on the guarantee chain.

Practical significance: a TIR Carnet is needed for the international transit of goods across borders under customs seals. It does not replace the CMR consignment note — these are different documents: the CMR records the contract of carriage, while the TIR Carnet covers customs transit.
