Road carriage of dangerous goods (ADR)

International road carriage of dangerous goods is governed by the European Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road (ADR) of 30.09.1957. Russia is a party to the ADR; the rules are also mandatory for domestic carriage (in accordance with Russian legislation).

Structure of the ADR

The ADR consists of two annexes:

  • Annex A — requirements for the goods: classification, the dangerous goods list, packaging, tanks, marking, documentation;
  • Annex B — requirements for carriage: vehicles, crew, equipment, movement, loading/unloading.

Classification (9 classes)

The classification of dangerous goods in the ADR coincides with the UN Model Regulations: 9 hazard classes (explosives, gases, flammable liquids, flammable solids, oxidising, toxic, radioactive, corrosive, miscellaneous).

Requirements for participants

  • Driver — a special ADR certificate (after training and examination).
  • Vehicle — approval for the carriage of certain classes (vehicle approval certificate).
  • Special permit — for high-consequence dangerous goods.
  • Equipment — fire extinguishers, wheel chocks, emergency equipment, PPE.

Documentation

During carriage there must be: a transport document stating the information on the dangerous goods (UN number, proper shipping name, class), instructions in writing for the driver, and, where necessary, a special permit and other documents.

Marking and hazard labels

Packages and the vehicle are marked with hazard labels and, for the vehicle, with orange plates (the hazard identification number and the UN number).

Practical tip: check that the driver holds a valid ADR certificate and that the vehicle is approved for carriage of the specific class. For high-consequence dangerous goods the special permit must be obtained in advance — without it carriage is impossible.
