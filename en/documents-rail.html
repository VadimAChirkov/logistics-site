Rail consignment documents

Two main documents are used for rail carriage: the SMGS — for international carriage between the participating countries, and the GU consignment note — for domestic, export and import carriage within Russia.

SMGS — international rail freight traffic

The SMGS (Agreement on International Goods Transport by Rail) has been in force since 1951 and applies to direct international traffic between the stations of the participating countries (CIS, the Baltics, China, Mongolia, North Korea, Vietnam, Iran and others).

The SMGS consignment note consists of five sheets:

SheetPurpose
1. Original consignment noteTravels with the goods to the station of destination and is issued to the consignee.
2. Road statementAccompanies the goods and remains with the railway of destination.
3. Goods delivery sheetAccompanies the goods and remains with the railway of destination.
4. Arrival notification sheetFor notifying the consignee, remains with the railway of destination.
5. Duplicate consignment noteIssued to the consignor — confirmation of the contract of carriage.

Key features of the SMGS: a single document for the whole route, recognition by customs, coding of goods under ETSNG/GNG, joint and several liability of the railways.

GU — the consignment note for domestic and export/import carriage in Russia

For carriage within Russia, and also in export and import traffic (within the territory of the Russian Federation), the rail consignment note is used. Its uniform forms are approved by Order of the Russian Ministry of Transport No. 191 of 19.06.2019 "On the Approval of Uniform Forms of Transport Documents for the Carriage of Goods by Rail" (registered with the Ministry of Justice on 06.09.2019 No. 55834; entered into force 365 days after publication). This order replaced the previously applicable Rules for the Completion of Transport Documents (Order of the Russian Ministry of Railways No. 39 of 18.06.2003, which lapsed).

Uniform form of the consignment note (four sheets)

The transport document — the rail consignment note of form GU-27u (GU-27u VTs) — consists of four sheets:

SheetDocumentIssued to / retained by
Sheet 1Consignment note (original)issued by the carrier to the consignee
Sheet 2Road statementfor the carrier and participants in the carriage process (at least two additional copies per participating infrastructure)
Sheet 3Counterfoil of the road statementretained by the carrier
Sheet 4Receipt for acceptance of the goodsretained by the consignor

Uniform form GU-27u (GU-27u VTs)

Instead of the previously used different blanks (GU-27, GU-29-O, GU-27u-VTs, GU-29u-VTs, etc.), Order No. 191 established a single form of the consignment note GU-27u (the machine-printed variant — GU-27u VTs) with identically arranged columns on all four sheets. All sheets are completed uniformly (by hand or by computer); the sheets differ in purpose (original, road statement, counterfoil, receipt), not in the content of the columns.

The GU consignment note is completed in the ETRAN system (Russian Railways) — electronic preparation of transport documents with an electronic signature; this is the de facto standard for domestic rail carriage in Russia.

The consignor completes the following columns of the uniform consignment note form:

Column of the noteWhat is stated

On the reverse of the consignment note, the consignor completes column 1 (stowage and securing of the goods under the Technical Conditions) and column 2 "Special declarations and remarks of the consignor" (agreement of open carriage, pallets, protection against freezing, attached documents, etc.).

Which document when

SituationDocument
Carriage between stations of SMGS member statesSMGS
Domestic carriage within RussiaGU (via ETRAN)
Export/import: the Russian legGU, then re-issuance to the SMGS at the border
Carriage to CIM countries (Western Europe)Combined CIM/SMGS consignment note
Practical tip: carefully check the cargo code (ETSNG/GNG) and the mass in the consignment note — the freight charge and customs clearance depend on them. Errors in the description or number of packages are a common cause of delays and claims.
