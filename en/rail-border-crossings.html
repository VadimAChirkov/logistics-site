Three tracks from China, or where China enters

While the eastern corridor Zabaikalsk–Manzhouli and the Kazakh pair Dostyk–Altynkol break cargo records and expand their tracks, the Mongolian route stays in reserve. We break down China's three overland gateways — and what it means for anyone moving cargo by land.

ANALYTICS · NOVEMBER 20, 2026 · Vadim Chirkov

Three gateways, three characters

China's rail links to Russia rest on three pillars, and they are far from equal. The eastern corridor — Zabaikalsk–Manzhouli — feeds the Russian network directly. The Kazakh corridor — Dostyk and Altynkol — brings cargo onto the 1520 mm gauge via Central Asia. The Mongolian corridor — Naushki–Sükhbaatar — remains a narrow transit window along the Trans-Mongolian railway. In essence these are three different “characters”: the record-breaker, the pair and the reserve.

Container trains regularly depart from China's largest industrial regions — Shanghai, Ningbo, Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Yiwu, Chengdu, Chongqing, Suzhou and Qingdao — and fan out across these three corridors.

Eastern record-breaker: Zabaikalsk – Manzhouli

The main land bridge between Russia and China is growing at an avalanche pace. By mid-July 2026, 15.2 million tonnes of cargo had passed through the Zabaikalsk and Manzhouli stations — 557.6 thousand tonnes more than a year earlier (Chinalogist). The average train transshipment time dropped from 10 to 8 hours. On the Chinese side, terminals were divided by cargo type: separate zones were allocated for mineral fertilisers, timber, grain, and bulk and break-bulk cargo. The container yard operates around the clock, using six gantry cranes. Over six months, 2,962 China–Europe container trains crossed the border, carrying 293,000 TEU in both directions (Rambler/Finance).

But capacity is hitting a ceiling. The crossing is currently rated at about 28 million tonnes a year, and it is being expanded: Russia and China have agreed to build a second main track of 1435 mm gauge. By the Transport Ministry's estimate, this will add 11 million tonnes by 2030 — to 39 million tonnes a year, or +39% (TKS.RU, 29.10.2026). The construction agreement has already been submitted to the State Duma for ratification (Russian Government Decree No. 1232 of 24.09.2026). The new Chinese-standard track will be extended onto Russian territory, expanding the transshipment infrastructure at Zabaikalsk station. According to the presented schedule, work on the Russian side is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, while the Chinese side plans to finish its section in early 2027.

28 → 39 million tonnes by 2030 — that is the effect of the second track at Zabaikalsk (+11 million tonnes, or +39%).

There is also a quick reserve without construction: switching Chinese infrastructure facilities to round-the-clock operation, experts estimate, could raise the crossing's capacity by more than 75% (TKS.RU, 05.11.2026).

The second eastern bridge — Nizhneleninskoye–Tongjiang in the Jewish Autonomous Oblast — is only gaining momentum: in the four years since its opening (November 2022), more than 20 million tonnes have crossed it, against a design capacity of up to 21 million tonnes a year. Russian Railways proposes doubling train volumes with its Chinese colleagues (EastRussia).

Oleg Belozerov, head of Russian Railways, meeting with China Railway management, proposed not stopping there and raising the number of trains over the bridge crossing to eight pairs, or 16 trains a day. Under the previous agreements with the Chinese side, traffic across the bridge is set at five pairs a day.

“New passenger cars, auto parts and a wide range of consumer goods (footwear, tableware, furniture, household appliances and computer equipment, etc.) are imported into Russia from the border city of Tongjiang. Exports are mainly coal, ore, aluminium and polyethylene,” said Elena Popova, press secretary of the Khabarovsk customs office.

The area near the crossing has broad potential for logistics and cross-border trade, note the Far East and Arctic Development Corporation (KRDV). Last year, a Russian government decree extended the Amuro-Khinganskaya ASEZ to the bridge area, covering about two thousand hectares. The Nizhneleninskoye investment site was created.

Its first project, a transport and logistics centre, is now being implemented on these land plots. In the future, various production facilities combined into an industrial park may appear here. An agreement with United Petrochemical Company (ONGK) is at the final stage with KRDV to build a complex at Leningrad-1 station for transshipping liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and petrochemical feedstock for further processing. Investment is estimated at 8.1 billion roubles.

Kazakh pair: Dostyk and Altynkol

The Kazakh corridor works through a pair of crossings, and both are breaking records. The oldest — Dostyk–Alashankou (since 1990) — set a new cargo turnover record of 18 million tonnes and met its annual plan of the Railway Cooperation Organisation ahead of schedule (Rail-news.kz). Modernisation raised the crossing's capacity to 28 trains per day (Kazinform, 21.02.2026; LEAN TRADE, 12.08.2026).

The second crossing — Altynkol–Khorgos — handled 10.8 million tonnes in eight months of 2026 (+3% year on year), of which 5.65 million tonnes came from China (+6%). In the container segment the picture is even brighter: 2,778 container trains, or 296,993 TEU in January–August (InfraNews). The entire Kazakhstan Temir Zholy network carried 1.234 million TEU over ten months (+8% year on year), while the Trans-Caspian route (TITR) handled 57.3 thousand TEU (+64%) (RK Transport Ministry, 19.11.2026).

18 and 11 million tonnes — the records of Dostyk and Altynkol. The Kazakh pair of crossings is already comparable in cargo turnover to the eastern corridor.

Mongolian reserve: Naushki – Sükhbaatar

The third corridor is the most modest. The Naushki–Sükhbaatar crossing on the Russian–Mongolian border and the Trans-Mongolian railway (Ulan-Ude – Ulaanbaatar – Jining – Beijing) carry a noticeably smaller container flow than the eastern and Kazakh routes. Mongolia declares readiness to expand transit: a new line is being discussed between the Altanbulag checkpoint (border with Russia) and Zamyn-Üüd (border with China) (Rambler/News).

For now it is more of a “backup airfield” than an independent freight artery: the route is used as an alternative when the eastern and Kazakh corridors are overloaded.

Scissors: 39, 18 and 11

Put the numbers together and you get a picture of “scissors”. The eastern corridor will reach 39 million tonnes a year by 2030. The Kazakh pair already holds two record crossings: 18 million tonnes at Dostyk and almost 11 million tonnes over eight months at Altynkol. The Mongolian corridor remains in reserve without comparable infrastructure.

Transit times, meanwhile, are similar: rail delivery of a container from China to Moscow takes 18 to 35 days, against 40–55 days by sea (EastLogistics, WhiteRoute). So the choice between corridors is less about speed than about capacity, load and reliability of the specific gateway.

What systematically adds time

Beyond the capacity of the crossings themselves, there are factors that regularly stretch delivery times and, at the same time, can be forecast (WhiteRoute). They are worth building into the schedule in advance.

— Chinese New Year (January–February): factories are closed for 2–3 weeks and transport infrastructure is overloaded — add 14–21 days to the planned lead time.

— Golden Week (early October): +5–10 days to production and consolidation times.

— Customs control: a complete document set (correct classification, invoice, packing list, certificates) sharply reduces questions at the border.

— Missing permits: if the goods require a TR CU certificate, SGR or an FSB notification and they are absent, customs issues a directive — this is at least 2–4 weeks of downtime; with questions on FSB notification or permits, the delay is entirely unpredictable.

— Seasonal freight peaks: in November–December, sea container rates rise and everyone rushes to ship goods before the holidays — and rail carrier slots become scarce too.

— SMGS paperwork: a “smooth” border crossing directly depends on the quality of the instructions you give your agent and the reliability of tariff-payer instructions. Treat this as seriously as you would an overseas trip.

What this means for trade participants

Three practical takeaways.

First, do not treat overland delivery as “one route”. Zabaikalsk, Dostyk, Altynkol and Naushki are different gateways with different capacity and load. There is no universally “best” one: the choice depends on which operator has confirmed a departure from the right Chinese terminal, whether there is a slot on the train and whether the border plan is agreed (RushHold Logistic).

Second, build infrastructure growth into your planning horizon. The second track at Zabaikalsk (+11 million tonnes) and the modernisation of Dostyk are a signal that the eastern and Kazakh corridors will keep absorbing rising flows. The Mongolian route is still drawing up plans — and it is best not to rely on it for time-critical shipments.

Third, watch the load on the crossing at the moment of dispatch. Cargo records are not only “good for the economy”, but also a risk of border queues in peak periods. Build a time buffer and keep a backup corridor.
