Air freight: conventions and rules

Warsaw Convention (1929)

The Convention for the Unification of Certain Rules relating to International Carriage by Air. It established the basis of the air carrier's liability, uniform requirements for the air waybill (AWB) and the presumption of the carrier's fault in the event of loss of or damage to cargo.

Montreal Convention (1999)

The modern convention that replaced the Warsaw system for most states. Key provisions:

  • Liability limit for cargo — 22 SDR per kilogram (unless a value is declared).
  • Stricter carrier liability and simplified rules for filing claims.
  • The electronic air waybill (e-AWB) is permitted.
  • Russia is a party to the Montreal Convention.

IATA rules

The International Air Transport Association (IATA) develops standards for cargo carriage: uniform AWB forms, cargo handling rules, e-freight and e-AWB standards, and requirements for dangerous goods (jointly with ICAO).

ICAO — Chicago Convention (1944)

The Convention on International Civil Aviation. It sets out the general principles of international air carriage. The ICAO annexes operate on its basis, including the rules for the carriage of dangerous goods by air (ICAO-TI, jointly with the IATA DGR).

Practical significance: for air carriage the carrier's liability limit is 22 SDR/kg (Montreal Convention). If the cargo is more valuable, the cargo owner may declare its value in the waybill — the liability then corresponds to the declared value.
