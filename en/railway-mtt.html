International Railway Transit Tariff (MTT)

The International Railway Transit Tariff (MTT) — Tariff 8100 — is an annex to the Agreement on the International Railway Transit Tariff (the MTT Agreement) of 01.01.1997, brought into force on 1 January 1997. It is the uniform tariff used to calculate carriage charges for the transit carriage of goods over the railways of the states participating in the tariff. The current version — as of 1 January 2026 — is published by the Organisation for Cooperation between Railways (OSJD).

What the MTT governs and who its participants are

The MTT establishes the procedure for calculating and collecting charges for the carriage of goods in transit over the railways of the following states (railway administrations):

  • Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Kazakhstan, the Republic of Korea, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Russia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Ukraine, Czechia, Estonia;
  • Mongolia (Ulaanbaatar Railway) — only for the carriage of loaded and empty large-capacity containers.

Scope of application

The MTT applies to consignments carried in transit over the railways of the listed states under:

  • the SMGS — the Agreement on International Goods Transport by Rail;
  • the CIM — the Uniform Rules concerning the Contract of International Carriage of Goods by Rail;
  • or other transport law.

Exception: the MTT does not apply to the carriage of goods to/from Vietnam, China, North Korea, Mongolia in transit over the railways of states where the Unified Transit Tariff (ETT) is applied simultaneously. For large-capacity containers to/from Mongolia this exception does not apply — the MTT applies.

The MTT also applies to carriage via border and port stations and stations where goods are transshipped to/from road, air, water and pipeline transport, as indicated in the tariff's transit-distance tables.

Structure of the Tariff

Tariff 8100 consists of six parts:

Part I. General provisions

  • Participants — the list of railway administrations.
  • Section I — scope of application, publication, the language of the tariff and the currency of the tariff.
  • Section II — the basic provisions on calculating and collecting charges: the procedure for calculating carriage charges, the name of the goods, the tariff distance, the weight of the consignment, rounding and the collection of carriage charges.

Part II. Calculation of carriage charges for goods in a wagon and goods on their own wheels

  • Section I. General tariff rules — charges for the carriage of goods in a wagon and goods on their own wheels.
  • Section II. Special tariff rules — charges for the carriage of individual categories: oversized and long goods, dangerous goods, cars in specialised car-carrier wagons, perishable goods, goods in a wagon not owned by the carrier, goods with a separate locomotive, household effects, animals, bulk goods, carriage with a reduced delivery period, etc.

Part III. Calculation of carriage charges for loaded and empty containers

Separate rules for calculating charges for the carriage of containers (loaded and empty), including postal items.

Part IV. Calculation of carriage charges for loaded and empty ITUs (other than containers) and road vehicles

Rules for calculating charges for the carriage of intermodal transport units (ITUs) — other than containers — and road vehicles (ATС).

Part V. List of dangerous goods

The list of dangerous goods to which the special tariff rules of Part II apply.

Part VI. Tariff distances, tariff rates, additional charges and other payments

  • Section I — tables of transit distances.
  • Section II — tables of tariff rates for carriage (in Swiss francs): in wagons, universal large- and medium-capacity containers, and empty wagons on individual railways.
  • Section III — additional charges and other payments.

Key rules for calculating and collecting charges

  • Currency of the tariff — the Swiss franc. Carriage charges calculated under the tariff are converted into the currency of payment in accordance with the national legislation of the country in which the charges are paid.
  • Moment for determining the rate — carriage charges are calculated at the rates in force on the date the contract of carriage is concluded (by the calendar stamp): at the station of departure — for through international traffic over the entire route; at the station where the legal regime changes — when the note is re-issued; at the transshipment station — when goods are transferred from another mode of transport.
  • Calculation procedure — the calculation is made separately for each consignment by each participant in the tariff.
  • Publication — amendments and additions are published on the official websites of the participants (for Russia — on the official website of JSC RZD www.rzd.ru and in the publication "Transport Rossii") and enter into force no earlier than 15 days from the date of publication.
  • Language — the tariff is drawn up in Russian; where the participants interpret the texts differently, clarifications are made according to the Russian text.

How the MTT, SMGS and ETT relate to each other

The MTT is a tariff instrument within the SMGS/OSJD system: it applies to transit carriage handled under the SMGS rules (or the CIM/other transport law). For carriage to/from China, Vietnam, North Korea and Mongolia via states where the Unified Transit Tariff (ETT) simultaneously applies, the ETT is used rather than the MTT. For a practitioner this means: before calculating a transit rate, one must determine which tariff (MTT or ETT) applies to the carriage over a given route.

In practice: carriage charges for a transit shipment are calculated at the MTT rates in Swiss francs for each consignment by each tariff participant, as of the date the contract of carriage is concluded. Exact rates and transit-distance tables are in the current version of Tariff 8100 on the OSJD website and in legal reference systems (for example, Garant).
