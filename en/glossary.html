Glossary of terms

Key terms of international logistics, sea transport and chartering. Freight-correspondence abbreviations are collected separately — in the section "Chartering abbreviations".

TermDefinition
Adjustment (ADJ)A correction of the freight rate depending on changing parameters (parcel size, season, conditions). Used in long-term contracts.
CharteringThe transfer of a vessel by the shipowner into the temporary possession or use of the charterer. See also "Bareboat charter", "Time charter", "Voyage charter".
Delivery basis (Incoterms)Delivery terms that define the point at which risk and costs pass from seller to buyer. See the "Incoterms 2020" section for details.
BarterAn exchange of goods without monetary settlement. Rarely used in sea transport.
Bareboat / Demise CharterA charter of the vessel without crew: the charterer takes the vessel into full possession and control, mans it itself and bears all operating costs.
Broker (ship / chartering)An intermediary between shipowner and charterer in concluding a chartering deal. Can be exclusive (under a framework agreement) or market (on a per-order basis).
Additional ChargesCosts of loading, discharging and handling cargo on top of the base freight.
GuarantorA person liable for performance of obligations if the principal debtor is insufficient.
General Agency (GA)A representation of a shipping company in ports, servicing its vessels.
General AverageLosses incurred through deliberate and reasonable expenditure or sacrifice for the common safety of the vessel and cargo; apportioned between all participants in the voyage in proportion to value.
Deadweight (DWT)The full carrying capacity of a vessel — the total weight of cargo, fuel, stores and crew that the vessel can take at its load-line draught.
DeviationA departure of the vessel from the contractual route. Unjustified deviation may deprive the carrier of the right to limit liability.
DemurrageA charge for the vessel's delay beyond the agreed laytime during loading or discharging.
Dispatch / DespatchA premium paid to the charterer for completing cargo operations ahead of the laytime. Usually 50% of the demurrage rate.
Additional FreightA surcharge on freight for special conditions (loading at an extra port, etc.).
Principal / CustomerA legal entity ordering carriage — the charterer or shipper.
EndorsementA transfer inscription on the back of the bill of lading by which an order bill of lading is transferred to another party.
CabotageCarriage of goods and passengers between ports of one state. As a rule, permitted only to vessels under the national flag.
Cancelling dateThe latest date by which the vessel must be tendered for loading. If the vessel has not arrived by that date, the charterer may cancel the contract.
Commercial Agent (CA)A representative of the shipowner on commercial matters, negotiating freight.
ConfidentA trusted person; an intermediate intermediary in informal negotiations.
Bill of Lading (B/L)The main document of sea carriage: a receipt for the goods, evidence of the contract of carriage and a document of title to the goods.
Laytime / Lay daysThe agreed period for loading or discharging the vessel, included in the freight. Once it expires, demurrage begins. The standard modern English term is laytime; "lay days" is the historical form, and in modern usage "laydays" usually means the start date of the window (in the laycan pair).
Liner serviceRegular carriage on a fixed schedule and route with published tariffs.
Lump sum freightA fixed amount of freight for the whole vessel or voyage regardless of the quantity of cargo actually loaded.
Dead freightA charge the charterer must pay for cargo space it reserved but did not tender.
MobilizationThe positioning of the vessel to the loading place under a voyage charter.
Multimodal transportCarriage of goods by several modes of transport under a single document on the responsibility of one operator.
Sea-airCombined carriage using sea and air transport — combining the low cost of sea and the speed of air.
Night SurchargeAn additional charge for cargo operations at night.
Notice of Readiness (NOR)A written notice by the master that the vessel is ready to load/discharge. The laytime count usually starts from its tender.
Encumbered vesselA vessel encumbered by a mortgage in connection with financing its construction or purchase.
Offer / Firm offerA firm proposal to conclude a chartering deal on the stated terms, binding on the offeror for the duration of its validity.
Particular AverageLosses that do not qualify as general average; borne by the party that suffered them, or its insurer.
Sub-charterThe transfer of the vessel to a third party (sub-charter) under a voyage or time charter.
Port chargesFees for the use of port facilities and services, included in the cost of carriage.
Advanced FreightPayment of freight (in full or in part) before delivery of the cargo.
Recap / Fixture recapA written confirmation of the main terms of a concluded chartering deal, exchanged by the parties before signing the full charter party.
Recap CorrectionA clarification of the terms after the recap when errors are found.
Voyage CharterA charter of the vessel for one or several voyages; the shipowner bears operating costs, freight is paid for carrying the cargo.
Seasonal RateA freight rate depending on the season (summer/winter period).
Certificate of RegistryA document certifying the vessel's registration and national affiliation.
Special ClassA vessel class for carrying heavy/special cargoes.
Urgent VoyageA voyage of an urgent nature, for which a speed premium is provided.
Standard TermsUnified terms of a charter party — GENCON, NYPE, BALTIME and others.
StevedoreA company or person performing loading/unloading work on the vessel and in port.
Shipowner / OwnerThe party to a charter party that provides the vessel for carriage and bears its operating costs (under a voyage charter).
SurveyorAn independent expert who inspects cargo and vessel, determining the quantity, condition and causes of cargo damage.
Time CharterA charter of the vessel for a defined period (months, years) with crew; the charterer pays fuel and port charges, the shipowner pays crew and vessel upkeep.
Time Charter Equivalent (TCE)The shipowner's daily revenue from operating the vessel, net of variable costs (fuel, port charges). Used to compare voyage performance.
TallymanA cargo counter who keeps a tally of packages during loading and discharging.
TonnageA vessel's capacity expressed in register tons; used to calculate charges.
Tramp shippingIrregular carriage without a schedule at freely agreed market rates.
FixtureThe fact of concluding a chartering deal, when the parties have confirmed all terms (clear fixture).
FreightThe charge for carrying cargo by sea, set by the charter party.
Ad valorem freightFreight calculated as a percentage of the value of the cargo carried.
ChartererThe party to a charter party that hires the vessel to carry cargo.
ForwarderAn organiser of cargo delivery acting as intermediary between the cargo owner and carriers, not owning transport.
House brokerAn in-house chartering department of a large company (oil, timber, etc.) working without external intermediaries.
Home PortThe port of the vessel's registration, determining its jurisdiction.
Charter Party (C/P)The contract for chartering a vessel between shipowner and charterer. Drawn up on standard forms (GENCON, NYPE, BALTIME and others).
Exclusive brokerA broker with a framework agreement with the principal, holding the exclusive right to find partners for its fixtures.
Express RateAn increased charge for urgent delivery under tight deadlines.
TEUTwenty-foot Equivalent Unit — a conventional unit equal to the volume of a standard 20-foot container.
FEUForty-foot Equivalent Unit — a conventional unit equal to the volume of a standard 40-foot container (2 TEU).
GRT / NRTGross / net register tonnage — volumetric units of a vessel (1 register ton = 100 cubic feet).
