Incoterms 2020

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) are international rules for interpreting trade terms that define which party (seller or buyer) bears the costs and risks at each stage of delivery. The current edition — Incoterms 2020 — came into force on 1 January 2020.

It is important to understand: Incoterms govern the transfer of risk and allocation of costs, but they do NOT determine the transfer of ownership and do NOT replace the contract of sale.

11 terms — two classes

For any mode of transport (7 terms)

TermFull nameMeaning
EXWEx WorksThe seller only makes the goods available at its own premises. The buyer bears maximum obligations.
FCAFree CarrierThe seller delivers the goods to the carrier nominated by the buyer at the agreed place.
CPTCarriage Paid ToThe seller pays carriage to the destination; risk passes when the goods are handed to the first carrier.
CIPCarriage and Insurance Paid ToLike CPT, but the seller also insures the goods (ICC (A) level of cover).
DAPDelivered at PlaceDelivered at destination. The seller delivers to the agreed place; the buyer unloads.
DPUDelivered at Place UnloadedDelivered and unloaded. The only term where the seller must unload the goods.
DDPDelivered Duty PaidDelivered with duties paid. The seller bears maximum obligations, including import clearance.

For sea and inland waterway transport only (4 terms)

TermFull nameMeaning
FASFree Alongside ShipFree alongside ship. The seller delivers the goods alongside the vessel at the port of shipment.
FOBFree On BoardFree on board. Risk passes when the goods are loaded on board the vessel.
CFRCost and FreightCost and freight. The seller pays freight to the port of destination; risk passes on loading on board.
CIFCost, Insurance and FreightLike CFR, but the seller also insures the goods (minimum cover ICC (C)).

The key logic of choosing a term

  • The closer a term is to the start of the alphabet (E, F) — the more obligations the buyer has.
  • The closer to the end (C, D) — the more obligations the seller has.
  • E/F-terms — the buyer arranges the main carriage.
  • C-terms — the seller arranges and pays carriage, but risk passes early (on handing over to the carrier).
  • D-terms — the seller bears risk and cost all the way to the destination.
Practical tip: for containerised shipments, FCA is often used instead of FOB — the container is handed over to the carrier at the terminal rather than "on board the vessel". This better reflects the moment of risk transfer in modern container logistics.

What else to keep in mind

  • Under CIP (unlike CIF) insurance must be at the ICC (A) level — maximum cover.
  • DPU is a new term in the 2020 edition (replaced DAT) and now allows delivery at any place, not only at a terminal.
  • Incoterms are always stated together with a place: for example, "FOB Shanghai, Incoterms 2020".
