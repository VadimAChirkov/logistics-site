Bill of Lading (B/L)

The bill of lading is the principal document of carriage by sea. It performs three functions:

  • Receipt — confirms that the carrier has received the goods in the stated quantity and condition.
  • Evidence of the contract of carriage — records the terms on which the goods were accepted for carriage.
  • Document of title — gives the right to dispose of the goods (it is transferred by endorsement).

Characteristics of the main types

Clean B/L

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading without remarks on the condition of the goods and their packaging.
When issuedThe goods are received in good condition, the packaging is intact.
Issued byThe master or the port agent of the vessel.
Financial consequencesThe bank is ready to finance under a letter of credit.
ApplicabilityAll types of cargo, the standard procedure.
Example"Received in good order and condition".

Practical use: for documentary credits (L/C), for insurance purposes, as evidence of receipt of the goods.

Claused B/L

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading with notes on packaging defects and visible damage.
When issuedPackaging defects are found, seals are broken, damage is visible.
Examples of reservations"Packaging in poor condition", "2 bales damaged", "Container dented".
Financial consequencesThe bank may refuse to finance under the L/C.
ApplicabilityWhen visible defects are proven.
Risk for the recipientThe consignee cannot conceal claims later.

Practical use: protecting the interests of the vessel and the sea operator, documenting visible damage, grounds for insurance claims.

Order B/L

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading transferable by endorsement to a third party.
Wording"To the order of [Shipper name]".
TransferBy the shipper's signature on the reverse of the document.
UseFor documentary credits and deferred payment.
Financial instrumentsOften used when discounting a bill of lading at a bank.
ApplicabilityExport of goods with conditional payment.

Practical use: financing export operations, collateral, transfer of title to the goods.

Straight B/L

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading issued to a named consignee of the goods.
Wording"Consigned to [Consignee name]".
TransferNot transferable — only the named consignee can receive the goods.
Risk of lossMinimal: only one person can receive the goods.
Financial instrumentsNot used under an L/C, as it is non-transferable.
ApplicabilityDeliveries to "established" buyers, domestic carriage.

Practical use: with a fixed consignee, domestic and adjacent carriage, maximum security for the consignee.

Electronic B/L (e-B/L)

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading in electronic form with a digital signature.
PlatformsTradeLens (Maersk), essDOCS, Bolero.
AdvantagesFaster document flow, lower costs.
Legal forceRecognised in most countries as equivalent to a paper one.
RequirementsBoth parties must agree to an e-B/L.
ApplicabilityModern logistics operations, containers.

Practical use: faster cargo clearance, reduced paper document flow, integration with supply chain management systems.

Through B/L

ParameterCharacteristic
DefinitionA bill of lading covering multimodal delivery.
RouteFrom the initial point of dispatch to the final point of delivery.
LiabilityThe carrier is liable for the whole route or assigns liability.
TransshipmentMay include transshipment between vessels.
ApplicabilityMultimodal carriage (sea–rail, sea–road).

Practical use: container carriage from inland points, door-to-door schemes, simplified settlement between carriers.

Practical tip: when working under a documentary credit, the bank requires a clean order bill of lading in a full set. A discrepancy between the bill of lading and the terms of the credit is the most common reason for a bank refusing payment. B/L originals are documents of title: send them only via reliable channels (courier, bank).

The rules governing the carrier's liability under a bill of lading (Hague Rules, Hague-Visby Rules, etc.) are in the "Regulatory framework" section.
