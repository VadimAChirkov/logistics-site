Commercial report and general-form report

The procedure for drawing up reports in the carriage of goods is established by the Rules for the Carriage of Goods and Empty Freight Wagons by Rail (approved by Order of the Russian Ministry of Transport No. 256 of 27.07.2020, registered with the Ministry of Justice on 16.10.2020 No. 60411) — the chapter on drawing up reports.

Commercial report

Purpose and cases of drawing up

The commercial report is drawn up to certify the circumstances provided for by Article 119 of the Russian Railways Charter (shortage, damage to (spoilage of) goods, discrepancy of the description/mass/number of packages with the data in the transport documents, etc.):

  • on unloading at public places — on the day of unloading the goods, and where necessary — on the day of delivery of the goods to the consignee;
  • on unloading at non-public places — on the day of unloading, with the goods being inspected in the course of unloading or immediately afterwards;
  • en route — on the day the circumstances to be certified by a commercial report are discovered.

If it is impossible to draw up the report within the stated time limits, it is drawn up within the following 24 hours.

By whom and where it is drawn up, completion procedure

The commercial report is drawn up by the carrier (at the station of departure, en route or at the station of destination). The report is made in three copies and completed without erasures, deletions or any corrections.

Distribution of copies:

  • at the station of destination: 1st — to the carrier for investigation; 2nd — to the consignee (recipient); 3rd — remains with the carrier at the station where it was drawn up;
  • at the station of departure or en route: 1st — to the carrier for investigation; 2nd — attached to the transport document and kept until the station of destination; 3rd — remains with the carrier at the station where it was drawn up.

The carrier makes a note in the consignment note on the drawing up of the commercial report at the station of departure, en route or at the station of destination, stating the reasons for drawing it up.

What is stated in the report (content)

The commercial report must contain, for each consignment:

  • the wagon number and type;
  • the number of seals (ZPU) and the seal markings;
  • the number of packages and mass of goods stated in the transport documents;
  • the number of packages and mass of goods actually present.

When the mass is determined on wagon scales, the gross mass, the tare mass of the wagon (from the stencil or verified on scales) and the net mass are stated. Information on overloading of wagons is stated in an annex to the commercial report.

Who signs it

  • The commercial report at the station of destination is signed by the consignee (recipient) or their authorised representative (under a power of attorney), if they take part in the inspection of the goods, and by the carrier or its authorised representative.
  • If they disagree with the content of the report, the carrier and the user of railway transport services have the right to state a dissenting opinion in it.
  • When wagons are unloaded by the carrier at public places (where the goods are delivered after unloading), the report is signed only by the carrier or its authorised representative.

Issuance of the report

  • At the request of the consignee, the carrier must issue the commercial report within three days.
  • Issuance to a representative — upon presentation of a power of attorney; to a natural person — upon presentation of an identity document.
  • If the carrier refuses to draw up the report, the consignee must file a written application within three calendar days (for foodstuffs and perishable goods — within one day); the carrier must reply within three days (for perishable goods — within one day).

General-form report

Purpose and cases of drawing up

The general-form report is drawn up by the carrier (or the infrastructure owner) at stations to certify the facts and circumstances provided for by the Charter: detention of wagons en route, presence of wagons on public tracks, a defective seal, signs of access to the cargo, a technical defect of the wagon, etc. The time limit for drawing it up is on the day the circumstances are discovered, unless the chapter on reports provides otherwise.

By whom it is drawn up and signed

  • The general-form report is drawn up and signed by one authorised representative of the carrier who drew it up (except for the cases in clause 65 of the Rules).
  • Reports related to the provision of public tracks and the use of the carrier's wagons are drawn up by the infrastructure owner (separately for the beginning and end of the detention) and signed by authorised representatives of the infrastructure owner and the carrier.
  • For carriage with escort and guarding by the consignor/consignee, the report is also signed by the person escorting and guarding the goods (if authorised).
  • If the user refuses to sign the report, a note of refusal is made in the report.

Number of copies

  • En route — two copies: the 1st is attached to the transport document, the 2nd remains with the carrier (a note is made in the consignment note).
  • For the collection of fines/charges/fees — three copies: 1st — to the document on the assessment of charges; 2nd — issued to the user of the services; 3rd — remains with the party that drew up the report.
  • On the discovery of a technical defect of a wagon/container en route — five copies: 1st — to the consignment note; 2nd — to the road statement; 3rd — to the consignor (or an authorised person); 4th — to the person repairing the defect; 5th — remains at the station where it was drawn up.
  • On a technical defect of an empty wagon presented for loading — three copies (to the wagon owner/consignor, the carrier and the recipient).

What is stated in the report (content)

The general-form report sets out the circumstances that gave rise to its being drawn up, including:

  • the presentation of uncleaned empty wagons/containers for loading (with the consignor's consent) — wagon numbers, the number and date of the contract;
  • detention of wagons en route — the reason and time of detention;
  • the discovery of a defective seal, signs of access to the cargo, defects threatening safety — information on the defects, the condition of the cargo, the seal and the loading;
  • damage to carried cars, road trains, self-propelled machinery, shortage of parts and assemblies;
  • the reasons and time of detention of wagons for the collection of charges for the use of wagons and public tracks;
  • the time of notification of the presentation of wagons, the time of presentation, the time of beginning/end of the detention.
Practical tip: the commercial report is the key document for filing a claim against the carrier for shortage of/damage to goods. Insist on it being drawn up immediately upon discovering discrepancies and check the accuracy of the information before signing — corrections in the report are not permitted.
