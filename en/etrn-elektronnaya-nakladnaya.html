A paperless consignment note, or the digital shift the market wasn't ready for

From 1 September 2026, the paper consignment note ceased to be the default document: mandatory electronic transport documents (EPD) came into force. In the first week the market issued 8.2 million electronic documents — as many as in a full year of the voluntary regime. Yet on the eve of the launch only 5% of participants were ready for the ETrN. We break down where the transition hurts — and what happens after 1 March 2027.

ANALYTICS · NOVEMBER 23, 2026 · Vadim Chirkov

Paper is no longer the default document

Federal Law No. 140-FZ of 07.06.2025 amended the law “On Freight Forwarding Activities” and the Road Transport Charter. From 1 September 2026 the consignment note, the transport order (request), forwarding documents, and air and rail waybills are, as a general rule, issued electronically and submitted to the state information system of electronic transport documents — GIS EPD (the system operator is Russia's Transport Ministry, the developer is FGUP “ZashchitaInfoTrans”).

The regime became mandatory for all participants in commercial freight transportation: consignors, carriers, freight forwarders and consignees. Paper is allowed in a closed list of twelve cases fixed by Transport Ministry Order No. 262 of 01.06.2026: military cargo; a foreign consignor or consignee whose details are not in the register of accredited branches; cabotage by EAEU foreign carriers; goods for the personal needs of an individual; a software/hardware failure or cyberattack (per GosSOPKA data); no internet access, including in settlements from Government list No. 1855-r; the consignor and consignee being the same person; precious metals and stones; heightened-alert, emergency, martial-law or counter-terrorism regimes; special cargo; periodicals; military and dual-use goods. A counterparty's lack of readiness for electronic exchange is not an exception.

The system's components are designed to distribute load and minimise single points of failure. The system is built for continuous operation, its elements are hosted in the “GosOblako” infrastructure of the Digital Development Ministry and allow horizontal scaling as the number of transactions grows (FGUP “ZashchitaInfoTrans” CEO Ilya Perevalov, mintrans.gov.ru).

Data from electronic transport documents form a transport-economic balance — a real map of the country's cargo flows. Thanks to it, infrastructure development can be planned on actual freight movement rather than outdated calculation models. This is the true purpose of the reform (Polina Davydova, director of the Digital Transport and Logistics Association, mintrans.gov.ru).

Over 48 million documents in four years of the voluntary regime — and 8.2 million in just the first week of the mandatory one.

The metamorphosis of readiness

The mandatory launch coincided with extremely low industry readiness. According to a study by the logistics platform ATI.SU, conducted in August among more than 2,300 road-freight market players, only 5.3% of respondents were already using or testing the ETrN.

A study by RAU IT and the TransRussia Connect organisers paints the same picture: 68% of carriers, forwarders and cargo owners either have not connected to EPD at all or use them occasionally; only 14% use all document types in full. As the main reason for the lag, 62% cite counterparties' lack of readiness, while 78% of those already connected issue no more than 10% of shipments electronically.

At the same time, the platform itself withstood the load. More than 448,000 enterprises became participants in electronic document exchange, and over 39 million documents were processed through GIS EPD in the first month (Kommersant). Polina Davydova, director of the Digital Transport and Logistics Association, calls this confirmation that the technology works on an industrial scale.

5.3% — the share of the market ready for the ETrN on the eve of the launch (ATI.SU, August 2026).

Where it hurts

The main difficulties are not in the platform's operability but at the junction between organisations. A telling problem is the unsigned title: a consignment note consists of parts signed by different participants, and if the consignee or warehouse delays its part after acceptance, the document hangs in an unfinished status and the whole chain stops. In the paper model such a delay was invisible; in the digital one it is visible to all parties.

It is hardest of all for a small carrier or individual entrepreneur to enter the system: they need connection to an operator, a qualified electronic signature, and an understanding of the document-flow logic — who creates the document, who signs it, at what point its status changes, and who corrects an error. By ATsTL's estimate, some small carriers and sole traders are still at the stage of choosing a solution and studying operator tariffs.

Geography plays its part: routes pass through remote areas, quarries and industrial sites without stable connectivity. Paper could be filled in on the spot; a digital document requires an offline scenario with data transfer after connectivity is restored.

There are also purely technical shortcomings. According to Danil Skvortsov, operations director of STD “Petrovich”, the redirection functionality appeared only in September, and electronic signature tokens turned out to be incompatible between iOS and Android. Operators have to configure roaming, draft exchange and the separation of EDO and EPD identifiers.

A separate block of questions is non-standard schemes: is an ETrN needed if the forwarder carries cargo with its own transport; how to formalise delivery not singled out as a separate service; what to do with partial acceptance and redirection. The market needs official clarifications from the Transport Ministry.

448,000 enterprises are already in the system — but for a small carrier the entry remains a barrier of an operator, a signature and the document-flow logic.

What this means for the market

The first risk is tax-related, and the non-fining period does not cover it. Until 1 March 2027, the Interior Ministry and Rostransnadzor limit themselves to a verbal warning when a paper document is presented instead of an electronic one (Presidential instruction list of 19.07.2026 No. Pr-1784). But the deferral concerns roadside checks, not the tax office: freight costs are recognised only when confirmed by proper documents (Art. 252 of the Tax Code), otherwise there is a reassessment with a 20% fine, or 40% in case of intent (Art. 122 of the Tax Code).

The second risk is administrative. There is no dedicated “no ETrN” offence in the Code of Administrative Offences yet; general articles apply: a driver without transport documents — a warning or 500 roubles (Art. 12.3 part 2); a forwarder outside the notification register — up to 300,000 roubles; a forwarder who failed to check the client's documents — up to 100,000 roubles (Art. 11.14.3). Dedicated fines are expected after 1 March 2027.

The third risk is structural. If unadapted carriers leave faster than a replacement is found, a shortage of vehicles will arise. By Skvortsov's estimate, some companies have already lost up to 70% of their previously available fleet, and the services of a law-abiding carrier can cost about 40% more — a direct path to higher tariffs for the consignor.

Questions the Transport Ministry has not answered yet

The Transport Ministry has issued an official handbook with dozens of answers to participants' questions, but the practice of the mandatory regime has produced a new layer of situations on which the regulator's position is not yet formulated. Here are the sharpest of them.

— Mutual recognition of EPD within the EAEU. A shipment between Russia and Belarus or Kazakhstan is an international one: it is formalised by CMR (the CMR Convention) rather than an ETrN, and the mandatory electronic format applies only to domestic Russian shipments under a contract of carriage. The authorities speak of moving to electronic documents with EAEU countries and “digital corridors” with mutual recognition as a task of the Transport Strategy until 2050 — first within the EAEU. When mutual recognition starts working and what becomes of CMR — there is no answer yet.

— Courier services and marketplace forwarders. Whether delivery services must register in the GosLog forwarders register — there is no separate clarification from the Federal Tax Service or the Transport Ministry; the question is resolved by the general criterion of whether the cargo was actually taken into possession and transport organised.

— The deadline for signing Title 3 by the consignee. The law sets no direct obligation or deadline for the consignee to sign acceptance — this is a legislative gap; responsibility for the delay can only be “extracted” through the contract.

— Self-pickup and Title 1. If the buyer is the party to the contract of carriage, while only the seller has the actual loading data, who creates Title 1 — there is no direct rule, and the seller is formally entitled to refuse.

— Responsibility when a counterparty is unready. If a participant in the chain has not connected to the exchange, has not signed the document or signed it with an error, how responsibility is allocated between the parties — there are no clarifications.

— No connectivity at the loading point. Clause 6 of Order No. 262 allows paper when there is no internet access, but how the driver and the warehouse should record this fact and transfer data after connectivity is restored — there is no unified procedure.

— The carriers register. What to do if a carrier's status changes after loading — whether the trip can be completed, what happens to the cargo and who answers — the rules should appear before the register launches, but so far there are none.

What to do before 1 March 2027

Connecting an operator and obtaining a signature is not enough — you must make sure the document passes the whole route and is reflected in the accounting systems. Practical steps: define your roles in each shipment (consignor, carrier, consignee — or all at once); check the Transport Ministry's exceptions; connect to an operator from the Transport Ministry register; prepare qualified signatures and machine-readable powers of attorney; agree on roaming with counterparties; arrange storage of signed ETrNs.

The next stage is the carriers register. Before its launch it must be decided what happens if a carrier's status changes after loading: whether the trip can be completed, what happens to the cargo and who is liable for breach of obligations. Otherwise the market risks repeating the previous mistake — introducing a mechanism and then working out the rules while it is already running.
