UCP 600 — Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits

The UCP 600 (Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits) are the ICC (International Chamber of Commerce) rules for documentary credits, Publication No. 600. The current revision came into force on 1 July 2007 and replaced the previous revision UCP 500 (1993).

The UCP 600 contains 39 articles grouped by subject. It is the set of rules that banks worldwide apply when handling documentary credits, provided the credit states that it is subject to the UCP.

Nature and purpose

A documentary credit (Letter of Credit, L/C) is an undertaking by the issuing bank, at the request of the buyer (applicant), to make payment to the seller (beneficiary) against presentation of documents complying with the terms of the credit.

The UCP 600 standardises this procedure: it defines the terms, the document examination procedure, the time limits, the responsibility of banks and the settlement rules. Thanks to uniform rules, the credit becomes a reliable instrument of international settlement, where trust between the parties is replaced by trust in the bank and in the documents.

Why and when it is used: a credit is used when the seller and the buyer are not prepared to rely on each other directly (a first transaction, expensive goods, political/currency risk). The buyer instructs its bank to issue the credit, the bank guarantees payment, and the seller receives the money against presentation of the required documents. It is used most often in international trade (in Russia — for import/export), and also domestically for large transactions.

Key principles

  • Independence principle — a credit is by its nature separate from the sale contract on which it is based. Banks are in no way concerned with such contract.
  • Banks deal with documents, not with goods (art. 5) — banks examine only the documents, their formal completeness and compliance, not the goods themselves, their quality or the fact of shipment.
  • Strict documentary compliance — the documents must exactly meet the terms of the credit; a discrepancy entitles the bank to refuse payment.
  • Irrevocability — under UCP 600 a credit is irrevocable, even if this is not stated in its text (art. 3).

Key rules of the UCP 600 (by article)

Examination of documents (art. 14)

  • The bank must examine a presentation to determine whether it complies on its face.
  • Data in the documents must not conflict with one another, with the credit or with the UCP 600.
  • The bank has a maximum of 5 banking days following the day of presentation to examine the documents and decide whether the presentation is complying.

Complying presentation and refusal (art. 15–16)

  • Upon a complying presentation the bank must honour the undertaking.
  • If discrepancies are found, the bank may refuse to honour (refusal is at the bank's discretion).
  • The bank must give a single notice stating the discrepancies and whether the documents are being held or returned — otherwise it loses the right to claim that the documents are discrepant.

Commercial invoice (art. 18)

  • It is issued by the beneficiary and its amount must not exceed the amount of the credit (unless the bank has agreed otherwise).
  • It must be issued in the same currency as the credit.

Transport documents (art. 19–25)

  • Bill of lading (art. 20), multimodal transport, air transport document (art. 23), road/rail documents.
  • The document must indicate the carrier, be issued within the required period and correspond to the mode of shipment.
  • A "clean" transport document — one without clauses about defects in the goods or packaging (art. 27).

Insurance document (art. 28)

  • The insurance document must cover the risks stated in the credit and be for not less than 110% of the value of the goods (unless otherwise stated).

Tolerances in amount and quantity (art. 30)

  • A tolerance of ±5% in amount/quantity is allowed where the quantity is not stated precisely (e.g. "about", "approximately").
  • Unless the credit states otherwise, partial shipments are allowed (art. 31).

Transfer of a credit (art. 38)

  • A transferable credit may be transferred to a second beneficiary (for example, an intermediary).

Practical points for a logistics specialist

Tip: when shipping under a credit, agree in advance with the buyer on the exact wording in the credit (description of goods, port of shipment, dates, required documents). Any discrepancy in the bill of lading, invoice or certificate is a risk of delay or refusal of payment. Most often, refusal is caused precisely by documentary discrepancies, not by problems with the goods themselves.

The full current text of the rules is in the official ICC Publication No. 600, complemented by the International Standard Banking Practice (ISBP 745).
