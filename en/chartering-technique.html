Chartering operations technique

Stages of a chartering deal

The chartering process passes through seven consecutive stages — from the offer to the final settlement.

  1. Offer — the shipowner or broker offers a vessel: its characteristics (type, tonnage, speed, deadweight), the route (ports of loading/discharge) and the freight rate.
  2. Negotiations — the charterer clarifies the terms: price, laytime, demurrage, despatch; rates may be adjusted and special conditions discussed.
  3. Recap / Confirmation — written confirmation of the main terms, exchange of recaps between brokers, agreement of the details and the final consent of the parties.
  4. Charter Party Execution — signing of the contract, use of standard terms (GENCON, NYPE, etc.), calculation of freight and advances, determination of the date the vessel is ready.
  5. Mobilization — the vessel proceeds to the port of loading, cargo is prepared, documents are completed, port services are notified.
  6. Operations — loading and discharging of the cargo, laytime control, issuance of bills of lading.
  7. Settlement — invoicing, assessment of demurrage/despatch, final freight calculation, payments by all parties.

Laytime

Kind of daysDefinition
Running daysCalendar days (consecutive).
Working daysWorking days, excluding weekends.
Weather-working daysExcluding days of bad weather.
Excepted daysExcluded days: holidays, strikes.

Examples of formulations:

  • "10 running days" — 10 calendar days.
  • "7 working days, Sundays and holidays excepted" — working days only.
  • "Weather working days" — days of bad weather are excluded.
  • "Laytime shall commence when vessel is ready, berth or no berth" — the days begin from the readiness of the vessel, even if there is no berth.
  • "Laytime shall commence upon receipt of notice of readiness (NOR)" — the days begin from the moment the notice of readiness is received.

Demurrage and Despatch

Laytime is included in the cost of freight. Exceeding the limit entails demurrage, and early completion entails despatch (a bonus for saving time, usually 50% of the demurrage rate).

FORMULAS:

DEMURRAGE = Number of excess days × Daily demurrage rate

DISPATCH = Number of days saved × 50% × Demurrage rate

EXAMPLE (overrun):
- Laytime: 10 days
- Actually worked: 12 days
- Demurrage rate: $5,000/day
- DEMURRAGE = 2 days × $5,000 = $10,000

EXAMPLE (saving):
- Laytime: 10 days
- Actually worked: 8 days
- DISPATCH = 2 days × 50% × $5,000 = $5,000 (saving for the shipowner)

Typical charter-party terms (GENCON 94)

  1. Parties — the shipowner (Owner) and the charterer (Charterer).
  2. Vessel — name, type, class, deadweight (DWT), permitted ports.
  3. Cargo — description and quantity, packaging and marking, prohibited cargoes.
  4. Freight — amount and unit of calculation, method of payment (Bill of Lading terms, Prepaid), currency of payment.
  5. Laytime — number of days, kinds of days (running, working, weather-working), start of the count (ready, NOR), excluded days.
  6. Demurrage & Despatch — the rate for overrun, the percentage of freight for early completion.
  7. Port charges — who pays (Owner, Charterer, split), the amounts of the charges.
  8. Insurance — who insures the cargo, the amount of cover (usually 110% of the value).
  9. General Average — joint participation in the compensation of losses.
  10. Liability — exclusions from liability, limits of liability.
  11. Force Majeure — kinds of circumstances and their consequences.
  12. Dispute and arbitration — choice of law, arbitration (London, New York, etc.).

Real example: a Contract of Affreightment (Gencon 94)

Below is a real example of a voyage charter party under the GENCON 94 form for the carriage of a consignment of steel by several bulk carriers. This is a classic Contract of Affreightment (COA) — a contract for a series of scheduled shipments, where several vessels and several lots of cargo fall under a single charter.

MARITIME COUNCIL (REVISED IN 1922, 1976 AND 1994)
CODE NAME: "GENCON"

Place and date: Moscow, 24.05.2026

Owners: Shipping Ltd., Edinburgh, United Kingdom
Charterers: First class

Name of vessel / Gross-Net tonnage:
  m/v "BALTIC SKY" or substitute — 2061/1178
  m/v "SONATA" or substitute — 5184/3046

Total deadweight on summer load line (about):
  3,085 t — m/v "BALTIC SKY"
  7,909 t — m/v "SONATA"

  1 SP Rotterdam or Dunkirk → 1 SP Vyborg or St. Petersburg

  in lots of 2,800 – 6,000 t each, jointly agreed

  Euro 12/t — Rotterdam
  Euro 13/t — Dunkirk
  Euro 36/t — Rotterdam
  Euro 25/t — Rotterdam

  and release of the bill of lading, to the owners' account (see box 23)

It is mutually agreed that this contract shall be performed subject to the
conditions of the charter party, which includes both Part I and Part II. In
the event of a conflict, the provisions of Part I prevail over those of Part II.

Part II — key terms of GENCON 94

The full standard terms of "Gencon 94" (Part II) include 19 clauses. The key ones are:

ClauseEssence
1. Presentation of the vesselThe vessel shall proceed to the port of loading after completing its previous obligations, load and deliver the cargo to the port of discharge.
2. Owners' liabilityLiability only in the absence of due diligence in making the vessel seaworthy; a list of exceptions (nautical fault, etc.).
3. DeviationThe vessel may call at any ports, tow, and deviate to save life and property.
4. Payment of freightFreight is paid on the loaded quantity; prepaid freight is deemed earned and non-returnable, even if the vessel/cargo is lost.
5. Loading/dischargingThe cargo is worked by the charterers free of the risk and expense of the owners (corresponds to FIOS); the provision of the vessel's cargo gear.
6. LaytimeLaytime runs from 13:00/14:00 if the notice is given before 12:00, otherwise from 06:00/08:00 the following working day; shifting to the berth and customs formalities are not counted as laytime.
7. DemurragePaid per day or pro rata; on non-payment — a 96-hour notice, then the right to cancel the charter.
8. LienThe owners have a lien on the cargo and sub-freight as security for freight, dead freight, demurrage and losses.
9. CancellingIf the vessel is not ready by the cancelling date, the charterer may cancel the charter; the owners must give notice of the delay and offer a new date.
10. Bills of ladingSigned in the "Congenbill 1994" form with the note "Without prejudice to this charter party".
12. General averageAdjusted in London under the York-Antwerp Rules 1994.
15. Brokerage commissionCharged on earned freight, dead freight and demurrage; on non-performance — 1/3 of the commission in favour of the brokers.
16. General strike clauseThe rights of the parties in strikes/lockouts affecting loading or discharge.
17. War risks ("Voywar 1993")The owners' right to cancel the contract or deviate from the route in the event of war risks (war, mines, piracy, terrorism, blockade).
18. General ice clauseThe master's right to leave the port in case of ice danger; the charterer's rights when the port of discharge is inaccessible.
19. Law and arbitrationSubject to English law, arbitration in London (or another option — New York / an agreed place).
What is important in this example: this is a COA (Contract of Affreightment) — a contract for a series of shipments of steel (~150,000 t) by several substitute vessels ("Baltic Sky", "Sonata") with flexible ports of loading/discharge and separate freight rates by direction. The FIOS L/S/D terms mean that loading/discharging and stowage are performed at the charterer's expense and risk — typical for bulk cargo. Laytime is set at a rate of 1,500 t/day with different counting regimes: for loading — WWD SHINC, for discharge — WWD SSHEX UU.

Practical advice for a chartering specialist

What to do

  • Be prompt — the freight market moves fast.
  • State the terms clearly — avoid ambiguity.
  • Check the documents — bills of lading, invoices, recaps.
  • Know the market — follow quotations, rates and demand.
  • Communicate through brokers — they are professionals in negotiation.
  • Document everything — confirm the terms in writing.
  • Meet the deadlines — especially laytime and payments.
  • Check the deadweight — do not exceed the vessel's DWT.

What not to do

  • Do not make promises without approval — first agree with management.
  • Do not ignore special conditions — they are important.
  • Do not be late with payments — it damages your reputation.
  • Do not sign a charter party without reading it carefully.
  • Do not neglect insurance — it costs dear.
  • Do not forget about laytime — it is money.
  • Do not economise on documentation — you will save on problems.

Freight calculation: examples

Example 1. Voyage charter

GIVEN:
- Vessel: General Cargo, 35,000 MT DWT
- Freight rate: $50/MT
- Cargo: Steel products, 28,000 MT
- Laytime: 5 days at 1,000 MT/day = 28 days
- Actual work: 35 days
- Demurrage: $3,500/day

CALCULATION:
1. Base freight = 28,000 MT × $50 = $1,400,000

2. Demurrage:
   - Excess days: 35 − 28 = 7 days
   - Demurrage = 7 × $3,500 = $24,500

3. Final freight:
   Total = $1,400,000 + $24,500 = $1,424,500

PAYMENT TERMS:
- 50% = $712,250 (on signing B/L)
- 50% = $712,250 (on arrival at the port)

Example 2. Time charter

GIVEN:
- Vessel: Tanker, 130,000 MT
- Daily rate: $48,000/day
- Period: 6 months ≈ 180 days
- Delivery: 1 September 2026
- Redelivery: 28 February 2027
- Off-hire events: 8 days of breakdown

CALCULATION:
1. Base hire:
   - Number of days: 1 September — 28 February = 180 days
   - Daily rate: $48,000
   - Base hire = 180 × $48,000 = $8,640,000

2. Off-hire (deductible):
   - Days without payment: 8 days × $48,000 = $384,000

3. Final hire:
   Total = $8,640,000 − $384,000 = $8,256,000

PAYMENT TERMS:
- Monthly in advance: 30 × $48,000 = $1,440,000/month
- For 6 months: 6 × $1,440,000 = $8,640,000
- After deducting off-hire: $8,256,000

Example 3. Container charter

GIVEN:
- Vessel: 5,400 TEU
- Rate: $1,200/TEU
- Cargo: 4,000 full containers (20-ft)
  + 200 reefer containers (within the 4,000)
  + 400 empty containers for repositioning
- Reefer surcharge: +15%

CALCULATION:
1. Standard containers:
   - (4,000 − 200 reefer) × $1,200 = 3,800 × $1,200 = $4,560,000

2. Reefer containers:
   - 200 × $1,200 × 1.15 = 200 × $1,380 = $276,000

3. Empty containers:
   - 400 × $200 = $80,000

4. Final freight:
   Total = $4,560,000 + $276,000 + $80,000 = $4,916,000

PAYMENT TERMS:
- Advance payment (25%): $1,229,000
- Balance (75%): $3,687,000
- Payment within 10 days of B/L
