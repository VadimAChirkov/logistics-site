Air Waybill (AWB)

The air waybill (AWB) is a document issued for the carriage of goods by air. Unlike the bill of lading, the AWB is not a document of title: it is not transferred by endorsement, and the goods are released to the consignee named in it without presentation of the original.

Functions of the AWB

  • Evidence of the conclusion of the contract of carriage by air.
  • A receipt for the acceptance of the goods by the carrier.
  • A document for settlement, customs clearance and insurance.
  • The basis for invoicing the carriage (rate, weight, volume are stated).

Types of AWB

TypeDescription
MAWB (Master Air Waybill)The airline's cargo waybill. Issued by the carrier (airline) for a consolidated consignment. Links the forwarder-shipper with the airline.
HAWB (House Air Waybill)The forwarder's waybill. Issued by the forwarder for a specific consignor. Links the consignor with the forwarder.

In consolidated carriage there is usually one MAWB (for the whole consignment) and several HAWBs (for each shipper within the consignment).

Particulars of the AWB

  • Issued in several copies (usually 3 originals — for the carrier, the consignee and the consignor).
  • The AWB number consists of 11 digits: the first three are the airline prefix.
  • It is valid for the whole route, including transshipment between flights.
  • The electronic e-AWB (IATA standard) is widely used.
  • For dangerous goods, the shipper's declaration (Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods, DGD) is completed.

Use

The AWB is used for all commercial air carriage. Unlike the sea B/L, goods under an AWB are released to the consignee immediately upon arrival — so air carriage requires fast clearance and is not used as an instrument for "holding" the goods in settlements.

Practical tip: for air carriage the rate is calculated by chargeable weight — the greater of the actual and the volumetric weight. Volumetric weight = volume (cm³) ÷ 6000. Take this into account when calculating cost: light but bulky cargo can "weigh" more.

The international conventions governing air carriage (Warsaw, Montreal) are in the "Regulatory framework" section.
