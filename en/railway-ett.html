Unified Transit Tariff (ETT)

The Unified Transit Tariff (ETT) is an annex to the Agreement on the Unified Transit Tariff (the ETT Agreement), brought into force on 1 July 1991. It is the uniform tariff used to calculate carriage charges for the transit carriage of goods over the railways of the states participating in the tariff. The current version of the tariff — as of 1 January 2026 — is published by the Organisation for Cooperation between Railways (OSJD). The administration of the Agreement and the ETT is carried out by the OSJD Committee (the depositary of the Agreement, Warsaw).

Who participates in the ETT

The ETT applies to the calculation of charges for the carriage of goods in transit over the railways of the following states (railway administrations):

  • Azerbaijan, Belarus, Vietnam, Georgia, Kazakhstan, China, North Korea, the Republic of Korea, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Moldova, Mongolia, Russia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Ukraine, Estonia — 18 states in total.

Difference from the MTT: the ETT includes Vietnam (VZD), China (CR), North Korea (ZC), which are not in the MTT; at the same time, the ETT does not include Lithuania and Czechia, which participate in the MTT.

Scope of application

The ETT applies to consignments carried in transit over the railways of the listed states under the SMGS — the Agreement on International Goods Transport by Rail, where carriage is performed via border and port stations and stations where goods are transshipped to/from road, air, water and pipeline transport, as indicated in the transit-distance tables (Section VI) of this Tariff:

  • a) between states where this Tariff applies;
  • b) between states where this Tariff applies, on the one hand, and states where this Tariff does not apply, on the other.

Transport documents

The transport documents for the carriage of goods in the indicated traffic are drawn up in accordance with the Rules for the Carriage of Goods (Annex 1 to the SMGS).

Structure of the Tariff

The ETT consists of seven sections:

Section I. General provisions

The tariff participants, scope of application, transport documents and the language of the tariff.

Section II. Publication of the ETT, amendments and additions to it

Amendments and additions are published in the official publications and on the official websites of the tariff participants.

Section III. Calculation and collection of carriage charges

The currency of the tariff, the moment for determining the rate, the calculation procedure (separately by each participant), the name of the goods under the Harmonised Nomenclature of Goods (NHM), the tariff distance, rounding, and the rules for charging by type of carriage: goods in a wagon, goods on their own wheels, ITUs (other than containers) and road vehicles (ATC), liquid goods in tank wagons, dangerous goods, animals, household effects, goods with a separate locomotive (the tariff does not apply), a reduced delivery period, etc.

Section IV. List of dangerous goods

The list of dangerous goods to which the special charging rules apply.

Section V. Additional charges and other payments

Additional charges and other payments collected in transit carriage.

Section VI. Tables of transit distances

The tables of transit distances used to determine the tariff distance of a carriage.

Section VII. Calculation tables of carriage charges

Calculation tables of carriage charges for the carriage of goods in transit over the railways of the ETT participants:

  • Table 1 — tariff rates for the carriage of goods in wagons;
  • Table 2 — tariff rates for the carriage of large-capacity universal containers;
  • Table 3 — tariff rates for the carriage of medium-capacity containers.

Key rules for calculating and collecting charges

  • Currency of the tariff — the Swiss franc, adopted for the calculation of carriage charges and additional charges.
  • Moment for determining the rate — carriage charges are calculated at the rates in force on the date the contract of carriage is concluded.
  • Calculation procedure — the calculation of carriage charges for a consignment is made separately by each participant in the tariff.
  • Name of the goods — all goods except dangerous goods are named by the consignor in the consignment note in accordance with the Harmonised Nomenclature of Goods (NHM).
  • Rounding — each charge and/or fee is rounded to whole Swiss centimes (half a centime or more is rounded up, less is disregarded).
  • Language — the tariff is drawn up in Chinese and Russian; the texts have equal force. Where they are interpreted differently, clarifications are made according to the Russian text.
  • Publication — amendments and additions are published in the official publications and on the official websites of the tariff participants.

How the ETT, MTT and SMGS relate to each other

The ETT is a tariff instrument within the SMGS/OSJD system: it applies to transit carriage handled under the SMGS rules. It is the ETT that applies to carriage to/from China, Vietnam, North Korea and Mongolia via states where the ETT applies. The MTT expressly excludes such carriage — where the ETT simultaneously applies, the ETT is used rather than the MTT. For a practitioner this means: the ETT is the "China/Far East" leg of transit (CR, VZD, ZC), while the MTT is the remaining transit (CIS, Europe, the Baltics). Before calculating a transit rate, one must determine which tariff (ETT or MTT) applies to the carriage over a given route.

In practice: carriage charges for a transit shipment are calculated at the ETT rates in Swiss francs for each consignment by each tariff participant, as of the date the contract of carriage is concluded. Exact rates and transit-distance tables are in the current version of the ETT on the OSJD website and in legal reference systems (for example, Garant).
