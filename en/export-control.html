Export control in the Russian Federation

Export control is a national system of foreign-trade regulation aimed at the non-proliferation of weapons of mass destruction (WMD) and their means of delivery. It restricts the import/export of goods and technologies that may be used for both peaceful and military purposes (dual-use goods). Unlike the EAEU non-tariff regulation (an economic measure), export control is a national system of the Russian Federation based on international non-proliferation commitments.

Legal basis

  • Federal Law No. 183-FZ of 18.07.1999 "On Export Control" — the principal law (subject matter, principles, methods, authorities);
  • Federal Law No. 73-FZ of 26.03.2022 — transferred the authority to approve the control lists from the President to the Government of the Russian Federation;
  • control lists (6 lists) — approved by resolutions of the Government of the Russian Federation of 16.07.2022 and 19.07.2022;
  • liability: Article 14.20 of the Code of Administrative Offences (KoAP) (breach of export-control legislation), Article 189 of the Criminal Code (illegal export/transfer of dual-use technologies), Article 226.1 of the Criminal Code (smuggling of dual-use goods).

The international basis is formed by multilateral export-control regimes in which Russia participates:

  • Wassenaar Arrangement — conventional arms, dual-use goods and technologies;
  • Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG) — nuclear materials and equipment;
  • Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR) — missile technologies;
  • Australia Group — chemical and biological weapons.

Control lists (6 lists)

Control lists are lists of goods, technologies and information subject to export control. Until 2022 they were approved by decrees of the President of the Russian Federation; after the amendments (Federal Law No. 73-FZ of 26.03.2022) they were re-approved by resolutions of the Government. A good falling within any of the lists requires a permissive document.

A separate regulation on the procedure for control applies to each list (regulations on control of foreign economic activity, approved by RF Government resolutions of 2000–2001). The full texts of the lists are available in ConsultantPlus and on the official internet portal of legal information (pravo.gov.ru).

Export-control mechanism

  1. Identification — the exporter independently determines whether the good falls within the control lists (by name, characteristics, TN VED code);
  2. Identification examination — where in doubt, the good is referred to an expert organisation (CEK) for a conclusion;
  3. FSTEC conclusion — on the basis of the examination, the FSTEC issues a conclusion on whether the good belongs to the lists;
  4. Permissive document — if the good is subject to control, a licence (Ministry of Industry and Trade) or an import/export permit is issued;
  5. Customs control — the FCS verifies the documents and releases the good.

Dual-use goods

Dual-use goods are goods that may be used for both civil and military purposes or in the creation of WMD. Typical examples: electronic components and computer equipment, machine tools and measuring equipment, chemical substances and materials, software and technologies, and certain types of telecommunications equipment.

The "catch-all" principle (comprehensive control)

Even if a good is not included in the control lists, export control applies where the exporter or the authorities have grounds to believe that the good will be used to create WMD or their means of delivery. In this case the FSTEC may require a permit to be obtained (Article 20 of Federal Law No. 183-FZ).

Practical exporter checklist: before concluding a transaction, check: (1) whether the good falls within one of the 6 control lists — verify by name, characteristics and TN VED code; (2) where in doubt, request an identification examination (CEK) and an FSTEC conclusion; (3) if the good is subject to control, obtain the licence/permit before filing the customs declaration; (4) remember the "catch-all" rule: even outside the lists, a good may be deemed controlled where there is a suspicion of use for WMD. The absence of a permissive document means refusal of release and a risk of administrative/criminal liability.
