Customs clearance

Customs clearance is the placing of goods under one of the customs procedures provided for by the Customs Code of the EAEU. The chosen procedure determines which payments are made, which documents are submitted, and what may be done with the goods after release.

1. Main EAEU customs procedures

The customs procedure code in the goods declaration (DT) consists of the direction of movement (EK — export, IM — import) and a two-digit procedure code. The classifier is approved by Decision No. 378 of the Customs Union Commission of 20.09.2010.

3. EX-1 / IM-4 — where they are used, where there are separate forms

EX-1 and IM-4 are codes of the European Union's Single Administrative Document (SAD). They are used:

  • in all 27 EU member states — the single SAD form (Union Customs Code, UCC);
  • in Turkey — a customs union with the EU, a similar system of codes;
  • in the United Kingdom (after Brexit) — its own CDS system, but the codes are inherited from the SAD.

Separate declaration forms in countries outside the EU:

  • Russia and the EAEU — the goods declaration (DT) with EK/IM codes;
  • the USA — Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501), the ACE system;
  • China — a customs declaration of its own format;
  • Switzerland, Norway, Iceland (EFTA) — not part of the EU customs union; national forms.

The correspondence EK-10 ↔ EX-1 and IM-40 ↔ IM-4 is correct in substance (export / free circulation), but these are different documents in different legal systems.
