Sea freight: conventions and rules

Hague Rules (1924)

The International Convention for the Unification of Certain Rules of Law relating to Bills of Lading (Brussels, 1924). The first universal instrument that established the minimum liability of the sea carrier: the obligation to exercise due diligence to make the ship seaworthy, a list of 17 exceptions from liability, a limit of liability and a one-year time-bar.

Hague-Visby Rules (1968)

The 1968 Protocol that modernised the Hague Rules: the limit of liability was raised (in SDR per package or per kilogram) and the scope was extended to containerised carriage. These are the fundamental rules referenced by most modern charter-party forms and bills of lading.

Hamburg Rules (1978)

The UN Convention on the Carriage of Goods by Sea. They strengthened the carrier's liability: the "nautical fault" defence was removed, the limits were increased and the time-bar extended to two years. They apply to a limited extent.

Rotterdam Rules (2008)

The UN Convention on Contracts for the International Carriage of Goods Wholly or Partly by Sea. A modern regime covering door-to-door multimodal carriage and electronic documents. It has not yet entered into force.

SOLAS Convention (1974)

The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea. Requirements for ship construction and safety. It includes the VGM requirement — the mandatory verification of the gross mass of a container before loading.

IMDG Code

The International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code: classification, packing, marking and carriage of dangerous goods by sea.

Load Line Convention (1966)

The International Convention on Load Lines defines the permissible draught and load capacity of a ship: the load line (Plimsoll mark) fixes the maximum loading, the permitted loads and the stability requirements. Responsibility rests with the shipowner.

UN Convention on International Multimodal Transport of Goods (1980)

The Convention on International Multimodal Transport of Goods. It defines the liability of the multimodal transport operator and a single document (CTD) for carriage by several modes of transport. It has been ratified by a limited number of states, but its principles are widely applied in practice.

Merchant Shipping Code of the Russian Federation (KTM RF)

The main national act in the field of Russian maritime transport (in force since 1999). It defines contracts of carriage by sea and chartering, the status of the bill of lading and the liability of the parties.

Federal Law "On Seaports in the Russian Federation" (No. 261-FZ, 2007)

It regulates the operation of Russian seaports, the procedure for port operations and tariffs for port services.

EAEU Customs Code

It defines the customs procedures for the import/export of goods, document flow and the procedure for paying customs duties. Since 2018 the EAEU Customs Code has been in force, replacing the Customs Code of the Russian Federation.

Practical significance: in practice it is the Hague-Visby Rules that most often govern the carrier's liability under a bill of lading. Check which revision of the rules your B/L or charter-party refers to — the limits of liability and the time limits for claims depend on it.
