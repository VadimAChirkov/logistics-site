Air carriage of dangerous goods

Air carriage of dangerous goods is governed by the ICAO Technical Instructions for the Safe Transport of Dangerous Goods by Air (Doc 9284) and the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR).

Key particulars of air carriage

Air transport imposes the strictest requirements on dangerous goods because of the risks in flight (pressure differential, temperature, vibration):

  • Many goods are forbidden for air carriage or permitted only on cargo aircraft (CAO — Cargo Aircraft Only).
  • The shipper's declaration for dangerous goods (DGD) is mandatory.
  • Strict limits on packaging and the maximum quantity per package apply.

Forbidden and restricted goods

Completely forbidden for air carriage (under any conditions):

  • substances capable of igniting or exploding under normal conditions of carriage;
  • many radioactive and infectious materials (except for certain exceptions);
  • spontaneously combustible and oxidising substances in a number of combinations.

Some goods are permitted only on cargo aircraft (CAO), without passengers.

Shipper's declaration (DGD)

The consignor completes the DGD — a document confirming that the goods are correctly classified, packed, marked and fit for air carriage. Without a correct DGD, the airline will not accept the goods for carriage.

Marking and hazard labels

Each package with dangerous goods must bear hazard labels, the UN number and the proper shipping name. For lithium batteries, special marking applies (UN 3480/3481).

Practical tip: for air carriage of dangerous goods, always involve an agent or service certified by IATA for dangerous goods (DGR). Lithium batteries are the most common "trap": their rules change, and incorrect packing/marking leads to refusal of acceptance.
